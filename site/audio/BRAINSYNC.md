## What comes from Wikipedia, not from Hemi-Sync

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemi-Sync

- "Hemi-Sync is short for Hemispheric Synchronization, also known as brainwave synchronization."
- Monroe's concept builds on binaural beats: two slightly different tones, one per ear, produce a perceived third beat; Monroe described the result as a "frequency-following response". Binaural beats are therefore **not stated on the hemi-sync.com pages I could read**; the link comes from Wikipedia and Monroe's patents (first patent 1975).
- Criticism recorded there: a 2002 presentation reported "EEG changes did not occur" with the standard electromagnetic headphones and concluded the effect was "electromagnetic rather than acoustical"; anaesthesia trials were mixed (partial replacement for fentanyl, "ineffective" for propofol). The article carries a note that it "may contain original research".
- A 2020 eNeuro study (https://www.eneuro.org/content/eneuro/7/2/ENEURO.0232-19.2020.full.pdf, found by search, not read in full) reported cortical entrainment at the beat frequency from binaural beats but no mood effect; reviewers describe the functional significance as an open question.
